\section{Empirical Evaluation \& Analysis}

In this section, we present detailed empirical evaluations of \textbf{Dynamic Activity-Dependent Pruning (DADP)}. We benchmark DADP against static and dynamic pruning baselines, evaluate dynamic optimization mechanics, analyze latent representation quality through information theory, and perform thorough sensitivity and ablation studies.

\subsection{Dynamic Optimization \& Self-Regulating Feedback Mechanics}
\label{sec:dynamics}

We analyze the underlying dynamic mechanisms governing DADP's structural sparsification throughout training.

\paragraph{Self-Regulating Negative Feedback Loop.}
Rather than relying on pre-scheduled pruning decay functions, DADP operates via an autonomous negative feedback mechanism. As optimization proceeds and training loss decreases, local backpropagated gradients $\frac{\partial L}{\partial y_j^l}$ shrink correspondingly across intermediate layers. On subsequent step boundaries $t \equiv 0 \pmod{\Delta T}$, importance scores $I_{ij}^l = \mathbb{E}[|a_i^l \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial y_j^l}|]$ for redundant connections fall below the global threshold $\tau$, triggering a localized pruning event.

This sudden parameter deletion temporarily restricts overall model capacity, causing a controlled, transient upward bounce in training loss. Crucially, this loss increase temporarily re-elevates gradient magnitudes $\frac{\partial L}{\partial y_j^l}$ across surviving active pathways. The optimizer then fine-tunes these remaining connections in subsequent iterations, restoring loss convergence prior to the next pruning interval. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:resnet_accuracy_loss}, this self-limiting cycle ensures the network only sheds capacity when current features have stabilized, recovering full classification performance with minimal or zero accuracy loss.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{plots/resnet18_accuracy_loss_comparison.png}
    \caption{Training loss and test accuracy trajectory under DADP pruning on ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10). Pruning events induce transient loss spikes that prompt immediate gradient-driven recovery, preserving final classification accuracy with minimal loss degradation.}
    \label{fig:resnet_accuracy_loss}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Emergent Structural Protection \& Channel Collapse.}
Although DADP evaluates connection importance $I_{ij}^l$ at an unstructured level, macro-level architectural preservation and channel-level structured pruning emerge organically without explicit layer budgets or group-regularization terms.

\paragraph{Adaptive Skip-Connection Protection.} In ResNet-18 at $99.0\%$ global sparsity, intermediate convolutional blocks are pruned aggressively ($99.5\%$--$99.9\%$ sparse), whereas branching downsample shortcuts retain $60\%$--$90\%+$ active capacity. DADP autonomously identifies skip connections as essential residual highways for gradient flow, protecting them from structural collapse.

\paragraph{Emergent Channel \& Neuron Elimination.} In VGG-16, DADP detects heavy projection redundancy in the initial classification projection (\texttt{classifier.0}), automatically pruning $82.8\%$ of its neurons ($\sum_j M_{ij}^l = 0$). Furthermore, entire convolutional filters shed $100\%$ of their connections, allowing physical filter compression directly on disk without accuracy loss. In contrast, static init-time methods such as SNIP fail to eliminate a single convolutional channel.


\subsection{Structural Modality Adaptivity: Vision vs. Sequence Networks}
\label{sec:layer_sparsity}

A critical advantage of DADP over uniform-sparsity algorithms (such as RigL or SET) is its ability to organically reallocate parametric capacity based on architectural paradigm and data modality, without requiring hand-crafted per-layer sparsity schedules.

\paragraph{Foundational Feature Lock in Vision Architectures.}
In convolutional visual backbones (VGG-16 and ResNet-18), DADP establishes a foundational representation lock on early visual layers. As detailed in Figure~\ref{fig:layer_sparsity_grid}a, early convolutional stages (Layer 1 to Layer 3 in ResNet-18) remain $100\%$ dense ($0\%$ sparsity). Because early spatial filters process raw pixel inputs to extract low-level edge, color, and texture primitives, localized gradient-activation products $I_{ij}^l$ remain consistently large. Pruning these early fields would destroy downstream spatial manifolds. Conversely, deep convolutional blocks contain extreme task-specific redundancy for $10$-class benchmarks like CIFAR-10, allowing DADP to prune deep feature maps up to $98\%$--$99\%$ sparsity safely.

\paragraph{Inverse Allocation in Sequence and Language Paradigms.}
In contrast, sequence architectures (BiLSTM-CRF and MiniBERT on SST-2) exhibit a distinct top-heavy capacity preservation profile (Figure~\ref{fig:layer_sparsity_grid}b).

\paragraph{Early \& Recurrent Projection Compression.} In MiniBERT, early embedding projections and intermediate attention key/query/value matrices are pruned aggressively up to $95\%$ sparsity. Similarly, in BiLSTM-CRF, recurrent state-to-state matrices (\texttt{fc\_hh}) are pruned to $92.5\%$--$94.2\%$ sparsity, identifying significant parameter bloat in context-free token projections.

\paragraph{Deep Decision Head Preservation.} Conversely, deeper attention projections, transformer feedforward blocks, and final classification logit heads remain remarkably dense ($75\%$--$85\%$ active parameters). Because error signals $\frac{\partial L}{\partial y_j^l}$ originate from class loss labels at the top of the network, high gradient activity concentrates within deep attention heads that map contextualized token states to task outputs. DADP's local thresholding mechanism automatically senses this top-level functional importance, preserving deep decision highways from structural collapse.

This cross-modal comparison demonstrates that DADP does not enforce a rigid, hardcoded spatial heuristic. Instead, it operates as a generalized structural optimizer, dynamically discovering whether a model's information bottleneck lies in its early feature extractors or its deep classification heads.


\subsection{Information-Theoretic Representation Quality}
\label{sec:information_quality}

A critical concern in deep neural network sparsification is whether aggressive parameter elimination induces representation collapse---a phenomenon where internal layer representations lose structural diversity and collapse into low-dimensional subspaces. To formally evaluate feature manifold integrity across layer depths, we analyze latent representations using matrix-based Shannon Entropy ($S_1$) and Effective Rank ($\text{EffRank}$).

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{plots/RESNET18_representation_entropy.png}
\caption{Information-theoretic representation quality across layer depths for ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10. DADP maintains high normalized entropy ($S_{\text{norm}} > 0.85$) and matches or exceeds dense baseline effective rank, confirming that activity-dependent pruning eliminates correlated parameter noise without collapsing manifold geometry (VGG-16 plot in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_full_tables}).}
\label{fig:resnet_entropy}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Manifold Preservation at Extreme Sparsities.}
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:resnet_entropy}, DADP sparse subnetworks maintain representational parity with uncompressed dense baselines across all layer depths, even at extreme sparsities exceeding $97\%$. Across intermediate visual blocks in ResNet-18, DADP preserves a high normalized entropy profile ($S_{\text{norm}} > 0.85$).

\paragraph{DADP as a Data-Driven Feature Regularizer.}
Intriguingly, in over-parameterized settings (such as mid-depth convolutional blocks of ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10), DADP sparse subnetworks exhibit a higher Effective Rank than the dense baseline. Because DADP evaluates pre-synaptic activations and post-synaptic error gradients ($I_{ij}^l = \mathbb{E}[|a_i^l \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial y_j^l}|]$), it selectively prunes redundant, highly correlated activation pathways that contribute no unique information to loss reduction. This confirms that activity-dependent synapse elimination acts as an online structural regularizer: rather than degrading the representation manifold, DADP actively purges correlated parameter noise, preserving latent feature diversity and explaining why high classification accuracy persists under extreme structural compression.


\subsection{Model-Wide Active Weight Distribution Spectrum}
\label{sec:weight_distribution}

To analyze how activity-dependent sparsification alters parameter manifolds compared to value-based pruning, we examine model-wide active weight value distributions across all layers of ResNet-18 ($99\%$ sparsity).

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{plots/resnet18_all_methods_weight_distributions.png}
\caption{Global active weight value distributions across all five evaluation methods on ResNet-18 at $99\%$ sparsity displayed in a single row. Classical post-hoc Magnitude Pruning and dynamic regrowth methods (RigL) introduce sharp bimodal exclusion gaps around zero, whereas DADP retains a smooth, continuous Gaussian spectrum.}
\label{fig:weight_distributions}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Functional Saliency vs. Bimodal Cutoffs.}
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:weight_distributions}, classical post-hoc Magnitude Pruning and dynamic regrowth methods (RigL) yield a sharp bimodal weight distribution. Because magnitude-based criteria select connections strictly via $|w_j| < \epsilon$, they enforce an explicit exclusion zone centered at zero, forcing surviving active parameters into symmetric positive and negative peaks. 

In contrast, DADP retains a single, continuous Gaussian-like bell curve centered at zero across all layer depths. Because DADP evaluates functional activity using the accumulated temporal expectation $I_{ij}^l = \mathbb{E}[|a_i^l \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial y_j^l}|]$, small-magnitude parameters carrying high error sensitivity during training remain active. Conversely, large-magnitude weights situated along silent forward pathways ($a_i \approx 0$) are pruned. The absence of a bimodal exclusion gap confirms that DADP tracks information flow rather than parameter magnitude.

\paragraph{Robustness Across Variance-Preserving Schemes.}
As demonstrated in Table~\ref{tab:init_ablation}, DADP exhibits structural and performance invariance across standard variance-scaling initialization protocols (Kaiming Normal/Uniform, Xavier Normal/Uniform, and Orthogonal). 

\paragraph{ResNet-18 Invariance.} ResNet-18 converges to an emergent sparsity band of $95.54\% \pm 0.16\%$ and a test accuracy of $76.29\% \pm 0.76\%$ across all variance-preserving initializations.

\paragraph{VGG-16 Invariance.} VGG-16 settles into a global sparsity band of $85.91\% \pm 0.94\%$ and a test accuracy of $85.19\% \pm 0.38\%$ across the same initialization schemes.

This stability confirms that DADP’s accumulated activity metric adapts dynamically to forward-backward variance transformations, eliminating the need to re-calibrate scalar pruning thresholds $\tau$ when altering network initialization frameworks.

\paragraph{Vanishing Signal Dynamics in Sequential vs. Residual Models.}
Evaluating unscaled fixed-variance initializations highlights a structural contrast between sequential and residual architectures.

\paragraph{Sequential Signal Decay (VGG-16).} Under a small unscaled variance ($\sigma = 0.02$), VGG-16 suffers catastrophic pruning collapse ($100.00\%$ sparsity, $10.00\%$ accuracy). In sequential networks lacking skip-connections, small initial variances cause activation $a_i$ and gradient $\frac{\partial L}{\partial y_j}$ magnitudes to decay exponentially across 16 layers. Consequently, at Step 0, $100\%$ of connections drop below the fixed threshold $\tau$.

\paragraph{Residual Signal Buffering (ResNet-18).} In contrast, ResNet-18 maintains performance under the exact same unscaled initialization ($\sigma = 0.02$: $95.96\%$ sparsity, $77.26\%$ accuracy). Identity shortcuts preserve gradient flow across deep residual blocks, shielding the network from threshold-induced structural disconnects even under sub-optimal parameter scaling.
